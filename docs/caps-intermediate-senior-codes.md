## How the codes work

Unlike the Foundation Phase (C.1, R.1, D.1…), the Intermediate and Senior drafts don't number their topics. We give each strand, grade and term group its own code:

**`<strand>.<grade>.<term>`**, e.g. `RS.6.3` = Robotic Skills, Grade 6, Term 3.

| Strand code | Strand | Hours a week: Grades 4–6 | Grade 7 | Grades 8–9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `AC` | Algorithms and Coding | 4 | 5 | 6 |
| `RS` | Robotic Skills (including mechanisms and CAD in the Senior Phase) | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| `IE` | Internet and e-Communication Skills | 2 | 2 | 1 |
| `AS` | Application Skills | 4 | 3 | 3 |
| `PAT` | Practical Assessment Task (week 9 of each term) | 2 | 2 | 2 |

`PAT.<grade>.<term>` can be used for lessons that build towards that term's practical task.

When the gazetted CAPS arrives, keep the codes where the topics match, and record every change in the version history so the build can report affected lessons.

## Notes for tagging

- Our Phase 1 cardboard lessons map mainly to **RS** structures and mechanisms: RS.4.3–4 (fasteners), RS.5.3–4 (pulleys), RS.6.3–4 (gears, linkages), RS.7.3 (wheels and axles). The Machines topics link the same way.
- Phase 2 micro:bit lessons map to **RS.4.1–RS.6.2** (LEDs, buzzer, buttons, sensors, DC motors) and **AC** (variables, if, repeat, operators).
- The draft's Grade 4–6 annual teaching plans name a block-based platform and a microcontroller, but no product. Our Blockly editor and micro:bit fit both.
