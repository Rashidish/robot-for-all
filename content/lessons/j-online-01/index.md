## Predict

Hold up a tablet. Ask:

- Which part of this can you touch? Which part can't you touch, but still use?
- Why do some apps and phones ask for a password?

## Run

1. The tablet, its screen and its buttons are **hardware**: you can touch them. The apps and the Junior missions are **software**: instructions that make the hardware do things.
2. A **password** is like a key: it keeps your things private. Share it only with a trusted grown-up at home or at school.
3. Walk paper **footprints** across the floor: everything we post or type online leaves a trail, a **digital footprint**, that others can see.
4. Practise what to do if something online makes you sad or scared: stop, close it, and tell a grown-up.

## Try

1. Open the mission **Bus to school**. The program you make is software; the tablet running it is hardware.
2. Plan the turns and repeats with a partner, then run it.
3. Talk: who else could see this program if we posted it online? Would that be fine?

## Make

Make a class poster with two columns: **hardware** (draw things you can touch) and **software** (draw app pictures). Add three "safe online" picture rules: keep passwords private, think before you post, tell a grown-up.

Colour a face and the stars on the mission sheet.

## Teacher notes

- **CAPS**: D.2 (passwords, digital footprint, staying safe), D.5 (hardware and software), D.3 and D.4 (computing devices and their uses), C.1 (developing instructions in the mission).
- Keep the online safety talk calm and practical. Children who share worries should be followed up with the school's usual care process.
- Never ask children to share real passwords, names or photos during the lesson.
