## Predict

Show the class a toy car, a cellphone, a spoon, and pictures of robots: a robot vacuum cleaner, a factory robot arm, a robot in a story.

Ask the class:

- Which of these are robots? Why do you think so?
- What does a robot do?

## Run

1. Together, sort the pictures into two groups: **robots** and **not robots**. Talk about each one.
2. Agree on a simple meaning: a **robot** is a machine that does a job by following **instructions**.
3. Point out that cellphones, spoons and robots are all **technology**: things people made to help us.

## Try

1. Tape a row of squares on the floor. One child is the **robot**. Hold up arrow cards one at a time; the robot takes one step for each card and does nothing else.
2. Ask: did the robot think for itself, or did it follow the instructions?
3. Open the mission **Three steps to the flower** on the screen. Children tap one arrow for each step the bee must take, then press Play.

## Make

Each child draws a robot that helps at home or at school, and tells a partner what job it does and what instructions it needs.

Colour a face on the mission sheet: how did it go?

## Teacher notes

- **CAPS**: R.1 (what a robot is), R.2 (kinds of robots), R.6 (mimic a robot from instructions), D.1 (what technology is), C.3 (carry out given instructions).
- Grade R is oral and practical: listen for "it follows instructions" and "it does a job" in children's own words. Use the observation checklist rather than marks.
- Keep each part to about five minutes. Act out the arrows again if attention drops.
