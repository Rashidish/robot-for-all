## Predict

When two gears mesh they turn opposite ways. Sometimes a machine needs the last gear to turn the **same** way as the first. A gear in the middle, called an **idler**, does that.

You have a **driver** (the gear you turn), a **follower** (the last gear) and two idlers: a small one and a big one.

Talk with a partner:

- With an idler between them, which way will the follower turn when you turn the driver clockwise?
- Will the follower turn faster with the big idler or with the small idler?

## Run

1. Print the **idler gears** template (check the square measures 50 mm) and glue it onto thick card.
2. Cut out the four gears, tooth by tooth, and the two board guides.
3. Draw an **arrow** on the driver and the follower, from the centre to the edge, so you can count turns.
4. Lay the board guide for the **small** idler on the base. Ask an adult to push a pencil through its three holes to mark the base, then make the three axle holes.
5. Cut three 4 cm pieces of skewer, push one up through each hole and fix it underneath with Prestik or tape.
6. Put a short piece of straw on each skewer, then the driver, the small idler and the follower, in a row. Check the teeth mesh.
7. Turn the driver slowly clockwise and watch all three gears.

## Investigate

1. Which way does the idler turn? Which way does the follower turn?
2. Line up the arrows. Turn the driver exactly **one** turn. How many times does the follower turn?
3. Now mark the holes of the guide for the **big** idler on the other half of the base and build the train again with the big idler.
4. Turn the driver one turn again. Does the follower turn more, less or the same as before?

## Modify

1. Fill in the table:

   | Idler | Turns of driver | Turns of follower | Same way as the driver? |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Small (12 teeth) | 1 | | |
   | Big (24 teeth) | 1 | | |

2. Take the idler out and mesh the driver and follower directly (you need a new hole). Which way does the follower turn now?
3. Predict: with **two** idlers in a row between the driver and the follower, which way would the follower turn? Explain how you know.

## Make

Design a toy where two figures must face the same way as they turn: a pair of dancers, two windmills or two rotating flags. Choose where the idler goes, draw your gear train with arrows for each gear's direction, then build and test it.

## Teacher notes

- **Timing**: 10 min predict, 20 min build, 15 min investigate and share. Precut gears for younger groups.
- **Big idea**: each pair of meshing gears reverses direction, so an odd number of gears in a row turns the last one the same way as the first. An idler changes direction and spacing, never the speed: the follower's speed depends only on the driver's and follower's teeth.
- **Common problems**: gears slip (axle holes too far apart: use the guide for the idler you are using); the follower turns the wrong way (count the gears: is there really one idler between?).
