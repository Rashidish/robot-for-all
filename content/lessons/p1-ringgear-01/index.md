## Predict

A **ring gear** has its teeth on the **inside**. A small gear can mesh inside it. In this build an **arm** turns on the centre of the ring and carries the small gear round, so it rolls round inside the ring like a planet round the sun.

Talk with a partner:

- When two gears mesh on the outside, they turn opposite ways. What do you think happens when one meshes **inside** the other?
- The ring has 36 teeth and the small gear 12. If the small gear rolls once all the way round the ring, how many times will it spin?

## Run

1. Print the **ring gear** and **ring base** templates (check each square measures 50 mm) and glue them onto thick card.
2. Cut out the ring gear: first round the outside, then the teeth on the inside. Cut out the small gear, the arm and the base.
3. Glue the ring on the base with their edges lined up all round.
4. Ask an adult to make the centre hole in the base. Push a 4 cm piece of skewer up through it and fix it underneath with Prestik.
5. Put the arm on the centre skewer through one of its holes. Push a short piece of skewer through its other hole, with a piece of straw on it, and put the small gear on it so its teeth mesh with the ring.
6. Draw an arrow on the small gear. Turn the arm slowly with your finger and watch the small gear.

## Investigate

1. Turn the arm clockwise. Which way does the small gear spin?
2. Line up the arrow with the arm. Turn the arm exactly **once** round the ring. How many times did the small gear spin?
3. Now hold the arm still and turn the whole base (the ring) with your other hand. Which way does the small gear turn compared with the ring?
4. How is this different from two gears meshing on the outside?

## Modify

1. Fill in the table:

   | What you turn | What stays still | Which way the small gear turns | Turns of the small gear |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Arm, once | Ring | | |
   | Ring, once | Arm | | |

2. Work out ring teeth ÷ small gear teeth. Compare it with your counts. What do you notice?
3. Predict: with a 9-tooth small gear (and a shorter arm), how many times would it spin when the ring turns once?

## Make

Put a pencil point through a hole near the edge of the small gear (make a hole with Prestik behind it), lay the build on paper and turn the arm to draw a pattern. Or design a model of a merry-go-round where riders spin as they go round. Draw your design and predict the pattern or the spin, then build and test it.

## Teacher notes

- **Timing**: 10 min predict, 35 min build, 15 min investigate and share. Cutting the inside teeth is slow: precut rings for younger groups, or use scissors with an adult for thick card.
- **Big idea**: an internal gear and its gear turn the same way. With the ring still and the arm turning (a planet gear), the small gear spins backwards as it rolls round: 36 ÷ 12 − 1 = 2 spins for one trip round, which surprises most learners. With the arm still and the ring turning, the small gear turns the same way, 3 times as fast.
- **Common problems**: the gear jams (the arm's holes must match the template exactly; check the centre hole is in the middle of the base); teeth skip (press the gear gently outwards against the ring).
- **Real world**: planetary gearboxes in cordless drills, automatic cars and bicycle hub gears; Spirograph drawing toys.
- **Machines link**: the Ring gear topic has the picture and a quick test.
