## How it works

1. The **pinion** (1) is a small gear on an axle. You turn it with a handle or a motor.
2. Its teeth push the teeth of the **rack** (2), a straight bar with teeth along one edge.
3. Each tooth the pinion turns moves the rack along by one tooth. One whole turn of a 12-tooth pinion moves the rack 12 teeth.
4. So **round** motion becomes **straight** motion. It also works the other way: pushing the rack turns the pinion. Car steering and some gates work like this.

## Try it

1. Mark the rack with a pencil line where it starts.
2. Turn the pinion exactly once and measure how far the rack moved, in millimetres.
3. Predict how far two turns will move it, then test it. Was your prediction right?
