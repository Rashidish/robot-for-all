## How it works

1. The **driver** (1) is the gear you turn, with a handle or a motor.
2. Its teeth push the teeth of the next gear. Meshing gears always turn in **opposite directions**.
3. The **idler** (2) sits in the middle. It passes the turning on, so the **follower** (3) turns the same way as the driver.
4. The idler doesn't change the speed. Only the driver and follower's teeth do: a big driver turning a small follower makes the follower spin **faster**; a small driver turning a big follower makes it turn **slower but stronger**.

## Try it

1. Mark one tooth on each gear with a dot of colour.
2. Turn the driver exactly once and count how many times the follower turns.
3. Take the idler out and mesh the driver and follower directly. Which way does the follower turn now? Does it turn faster, slower or the same?
